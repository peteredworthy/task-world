The 60-second read

Enough structure to decide whether the process is sound. Increase detail only when you need rationale, evidence, or verification.

Structural diagnosis

The previous process prohibited screens, optimized for catalogs and validators, delayed visual feedback, and turned uncertainty into a global prerequisite. Useful research survived; the machinery did not.

Replacement principle

Research just enough to make one visible screen claim honest. Review the screen, then let observed design needs pull more research.

The shape of the work

6 process stages7 linked concept states3 deliverables1 scenario: r3141 challenge1 refinement pass

Stages: Freeze Scenario → Build Graybox → Review Operator Flow → Pull Targeted Research → Reality Challenge → Refine & Evaluate. States: Fleet → Graph Workspace → Failure Comparison → Node Audit → Intervention Decision → Proposed Steering → Recovery & Postscript.

Three outputs only

A scenario sheet, one offline clickable HTML concept, and a review record. No catalog, schema, validator, generator, snapshot, or feedback importer.

Research & refinement stops

Every iteration changes a screen or stops. Research stops when either answer leaves the deck unchanged. Refinement stops after one challenge and one response pass; then decide.

Concept rejection gates

Reject or substantially revise when decision readiness, evidence access, action safety, or capability honesty scores below 3. Also reject capability leakage, continuity loss, acceptance-only feedback, or narrow-layout loss of decision-critical information.

Six-stage loop

From scenario to decision

Front-load the reviewable flow. Research is a pull system, not a gate in front of design.

Freeze the scenario

One degrading run, r314, among four runs. Record questions, realistic data, capability status, citations, and known gaps.

Build graybox now

Link all seven low-fidelity states before polishing any screen. The flow is the first useful artifact.

Review operator flow

Walk rubric tasks. Note wrong inferences, missing information, resets, backtracks, ambiguity, and unconfirmed outcomes.

Pull targeted research

Start with decisions and reports. Reopen code or tests only for an ambiguous field, state, relation, transition, or action.

Reality challenge

A second reader challenges only claims capable of materially changing the deck, then edits findings in place.

Refine once, evaluate

Apply focused findings, make one refinement pass, score the concept, and choose continue, revise, combine, or stop.

“Every iteration must change a reviewable screen or stop.”
Evidence economy

Research enters at the point of use

Record the answer in the scenario note and update the affected screen. Create no separate research artifact unless the deck directly consumes it.

Rationale · why pull beats inventory

It bounds uncertainty to an actual decision and prevents a second canonical model from accumulating invented states, conflated outputs, and broken cross-references.

Review discipline

Challenge material claims, not everything

Prioritize identity and scope errors, invented behavior, optimistic success, causal overclaiming, capability leakage, and misleading freshness, cost, authority, or coverage.

Design fixture

One degrading run, in context

The versioned scenario fixture is specific enough to make screens honest—not generalized enough to become a domain model.

Fleet · r311–r314

Four runs establish contrast

Each includes status, frontier, last event, pending human action, active lease, final gate, recorded usage, and pricing coverage. Quiet runs use observed facts—not a fictitious “healthy” classifier.

Focus · r314

Six to eight connected nodes

Two worker candidates, two failed verifier reports, a human gate or corrective planning point, and a final gate blocked by one unresolved requirement.

Required texture

Enough evidence to judge a retry

Directed edges, required bindings, scheduler buckets, states, leases, repeated requirement, two verifier reasons, candidate records, file-state boundaries, prompt-summary metadata, ordered events, and two usage facts—one unpriced.

Action contrast

Executable now vs imagined next

Include one currently executable decision and a separately labeled proposed typed-steering path. Proposed steering must never look operable in the current product.

CurrentPersisted or executable behavior available in the product now.
DerivedAn interpretation computed from current evidence; useful, but not a persisted fact or detector.
ProposedA future concept for coherence and desirability testing; not currently available or executable.
Error states · variants the scenario must expose
  • Accepted but not yet applied, followed by projection-confirmed success.
  • Stale proposal or graph base; validation rejection with reason.
  • Unavailable or partial evidence; unpriced usage.
  • Disconnected event stream—“Live” means connection only.
  • Action unavailable in current state, including Cancel while stopping.

Seven-state journey

The operator’s questions stay in motion

A horizontal strip makes the whole decision journey inspectable at once, then stacks into a scan-friendly sequence on narrow screens.

1

Fleet

Where is attention needed?

Quiet sweep; rank r314 by observed conditions. Preserve filters and return context.

2

Graph Workspace

Where is the run blocked?

Frontier, expansion, bindings, downstream topology, final gate; selection is one run-scoped node_id. Graph nodes are not legacy tasks.

3

Failure Comparison

What repeated—and what changed?

Compare requirement, reasons, candidates, file boundaries, and scoped cost across attempts. Raw differences are current evidence; retry information value is not a current detector.

4

Node Audit

What is the ground truth?

Hold selection steady while exposing records, outputs, trace, prompt summary, files, and usage.

5

Intervention Decision

What can I safely do now?

Current Gate, patch, retry, or lifecycle actions expose trigger, scope, evidence, consequence, validation, reversibility, and provenance.

6

Proposed Steering

Would typed steering resolve it?

Proposed Instruction, context, evidence binding, budget, validation, and delivery proof—visibly non-executable.

7

Recovery & Postscript

Did corrective work actually begin?

“Accepted, applying…” until projection confirmation; then postscript. Cohort analysis remains proposed.

Continuity contract

run identity + synopsisselected node + evidenceattempt / timeunresolved decision / actionfreshness + capability honestysource + return context

Desktop can place context beside focus; narrow views stack below a sticky context strip. Neither may remove information needed to authorize action.

Evidence stays close

Raw prompts, transcripts, tool activity, diffs, and full topology open through progressive disclosure—not context-resetting destinations. Missing exact prompts, partial traces, and non-causal file-state evidence stay visible.

Actions remain honest

Attribution labels do not imply authenticated identity. Consequential actions use a proper modal. HTTP acceptance is never rendered as success before the projection confirms it.

Two-track evaluation

Judge reality and possibility separately

The same deck can test current decision support and future coherence without letting proposed capability leak into current claims.

Current-grounded

Tasks 1–6

  1. Gain quiet-fleet confidence.
  2. Find the run needing attention.
  3. Locate its graph position.
  4. Explain repeated failure.
  5. Judge retry value from available evidence.
  6. Inspect ground truth.
Future concept

Tasks 7–9

  1. Explore proposed typed steering.
  2. Prove intervention reached corrective work.
  3. Compare across runs.

Scores coherence and desirability—not implementation readiness.

Reject below the honesty floor

Substantially revise if decision readiness, evidence access, action safety, or capability honesty scores below 3.

Reject broken continuity or feedback

Reject if a lens loses context, proposed/derived looks current, feedback stops at acceptance, or narrow layout hides decision-critical information.

Exact verification steps
  1. Open the concept HTML through file:// with network access unavailable.
  2. Exercise every link, disclosure, and action-result variant.
  3. Inspect representative desktop and narrow viewports.
  4. Complete the JTBD rubric walkthrough and record observations.
  5. Confirm every visible nontrivial claim is cited in the scenario sheet or clearly labeled derived or proposed.

Automated browser tests are optional when interaction complexity makes regression risk material; they are not a prerequisite for design learning.

Stop conditions

Bound the work before it becomes a system

The process succeeds by producing a decision, not by maximizing research or artifact count.

  • Stop research when either possible answer would leave the deck unchanged.
  • Stop when uncertainty can be omitted or honestly labeled.
  • Stop when resolution requires unrelated backend or future-product design.
  • Stop when one research pass does not change a screen.
  • Stop expanding when a proposed file is not directly consumed by the concept or its evaluation.
  • Stop refining after one focused challenge and one response pass; choose continue, revise, combine, or stop.
